The PMG Compiler

A knowledge compiler, not a perception pipeline. It transforms one messy demonstration into an explicit procedural memory — coarse to fine to hierarchical — following one principle: expand first, compress later.

Passes

Three passes, coarse to fine

1
Online · lightweight

Provisional Global Spine

During recording, a lightweight LLM proposes coarse milestone hypotheses — semantic guesses about the task's major checkpoints. These aren't the final milestones; they guide graph construction and are corrected later against full evidence.

2
Offline · expansion

Procedural Expansion → Intermediate Representation

Offline, the compiler reconstructs the richest possible Local Execution Graph — Procedural States with their world representations, parameterized actions, observable conditions, and exit criteria. This is the compiler's IR: it deliberately over-captures before anything is compressed away.

3
Hierarchical · compression

Hierarchical Semantic Compression

The compiler validates, renames, splits, merges, and inserts milestones — deriving a stable milestone backbone from the reconstructed execution graphs. Milestones emerge here as semantic abstractions over completed execution graphs, not as things extracted directly from video.

Algorithms

What the passes actually run

These are the compiler's core algorithms. Two of them are the highest-priority open problems in the whole project — because removing special recovery constructs makes everything hinge on one question: "have I seen this world state before?"

State Identity priority

When are two observed world configurations the same Procedural State? Governs loops, demonstration merging, and graph size. Because the PMG is egocentric, matching must be viewpoint-tolerant — the planned resolution is a PMG Perception Encoder whose latent space is organized around procedural equivalence rather than visual similarity.

State Segmentation priority

When should the compiler emit a new state instead of extending the current one? Defines the boundaries of the Local Execution Graph — and is fundamentally event-driven: new nodes only on meaningful procedural events, never from time passing alone.

Demonstration Merging

Fold multiple demonstrations into one memory — partly a registry-alignment problem — accumulating canonical procedures, legitimate alternatives, and recovery loops.

Semantic Compression

Collapse the expanded IR into stable milestones: rename, split, merge, and insert to produce the final hierarchy.

Procedural Relevance Filter

Operating inside a known Task / Milestone / State, the compiler asks only "what is relevant for progressing to the next state?" — discarding fidgeting, camera motion, and unrelated scene changes. This prevents graph explosion without aggressive post-processing.

Milestone Validation

Check the provisional spine against reconstructed evidence and reorganize it — the coarse online guesses are confirmed or replaced.

Intermediate Representation

The Local Execution Graph is the IR

Like any compiler, PMG has an intermediate representation between the raw input and the final artifact. Here it is the Local Execution Graph: the richest procedural information available before semantic compression.

Over-capture first

The IR intentionally stores more than the final graph needs — full state detail, every observed action, all candidate conditions. Nothing is thrown away until Pass 3 has the complete picture.

Evidence for milestones

Stable High-Level Milestones and the final PMG hierarchy are derived from the IR. Milestones are compression over the reconstructed execution graphs — so the IR is the evidence that justifies them.

This ordering — expand into the IR, then compress into hierarchy — is what "Expand First, Compress Later" means concretely. The IR makes the compressions defensible: every milestone can point back to the procedural evidence it summarizes.

Evidence

Grounded in a real demonstration

One real 10-minute demonstration — desoldering a header, including a genuine failure and strategy switch — is the source material the compiler consumes. Every Procedural State references the frames it was compiled from.

Kept — procedurally relevant

Intentional actions, world changes, contact changes, object interactions, state verification, alternatives, recovery, and anything that influences future execution.

Discarded — incidental

Background movement, human fidgeting, unrelated object motion, camera shake, irrelevant conversation — anything not needed to reproduce the task.
