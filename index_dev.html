The thesis
Procedures should be stored as explicit world evolution, not implicit trajectories or model weights.
The Problem

How do you store "how to do something" so anyone can execute it?

A demonstration is ephemeral: it records what happened once, from one viewpoint, with all the incidental fidgeting. Humans don't remember procedures that way — we compress experience into meaningful milestones, keep the decisions that matter, discard the rest, and reconstruct the procedure when we perform it again.

We want machines to do the same: turn a demonstration into procedural knowledge that is interpretable, editable, mergeable across demonstrations, recoverable from failure, and transferable across bodies. That knowledge should carry enough physical detail — geometry, contacts, timing, operating points — to actually be executed, while staying a structure a person can read and correct.

Videos are memory.
Videos are observations.
World evolution is memory. PMG stores world evolution.

Why PMG

What procedural memory needs

The design philosophy in one line: separate procedural knowledge from the reasoning system. The graph stores the procedure; reasoning systems consume it rather than implicitly memorizing it. That separation is what makes the following possible.

Explicit & editable

Interpretable, verifiable, correctable by an expert — not opaque weights or a black-box policy.

World-centric

Nodes are meaningful world states, not trajectories. Procedures are modeled as changes in the world.

Mergeable

Many demonstrations accumulate into one memory of the procedure — with alternatives and recovery paths.

Persistent

Knowledge lives outside the model and survives across sessions, tasks, and model upgrades.

Embodiment-agnostic

Stores intent and operating points, never one body's motion — so it guides a human or a humanoid.

Procedurally relevant

Only information needed to reproduce the task is kept; incidental motion and background are discarded.

Overall Architecture

Two stages build it — one engine runs it

A Compiler turns demonstrations into the graph; the Graph is the persistent memory; a Procedural Reasoning Engine consumes it at runtime to guide whichever executor is present.

Runtime — the Procedural Reasoning Engine

Every runtime decision is grounded in the graph. The engine localizes the current world state, decides the next step, requests perception verification, and guides the executor. Today it may be an LLM; tomorrow a specialized model — the architecture doesn't care.
